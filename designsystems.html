Overview

A design system isn't just a library of components.

Done well, it's an operating system for product teams. It creates consistency for customers, gives engineering a shared language, and enables organisations to move faster without sacrificing quality.

Across multiple enterprise organisations, I've helped evolve design systems beyond UI libraries into products that support thousands of users, multiple brands and teams distributed across different countries.

The two engagements that follow sit at different points on that spectrum. Native Instruments needed a multi-brand system built to support a fast-growing portfolio of acquisitions. Forrit needed one evolved within the tighter constraints of an existing component library, Material UI. Both needed the same thing underneath: a system that let the organisation move faster without losing consistency or quality.

Project 01 — Native Instruments

Scaling a global ecommerce platform across multiple brands.

Initially brought in to conduct a UX audit of the Native Instruments webshop, the engagement quickly expanded into a long-term design partnership spanning more than four years.

The original project focused on identifying friction throughout the purchasing journey — analysing customer behaviour, uncovering usability barriers and redesigning key parts of the experience to improve conversion and reduce unnecessary complexity.

The System

From component library to enterprise design system.

As Native Instruments migrated to a custom CMS, my role evolved from solving individual UX problems to helping define the design system that would underpin multiple brands, teams and products across the company's growing portfolio.

The goal wasn't to create another component library. It was to build a shared foundation that allowed every brand to maintain its own identity while giving product and engineering teams consistent patterns, accessibility standards and reusable building blocks.

Publishing tools for a global partner network

As Native Instruments grew, its customer support teams were spending an increasing amount of time managing software and instrument updates on behalf of third-party developers. Valuable time and resources were being diverted away from helping customers and into administrative work — at a real cost to the business.

Working closely with stakeholders, product managers and engineering across Germany, I helped design a new self-service publishing platform that let third-party partners fully manage their own products — software, updates and content — through a purpose-built workflow, rather than relying on internal teams to publish on their behalf.

The project involved discovery workshops, stakeholder interviews, user journey mapping and usability testing to understand the needs of both internal teams and external partners — balancing business objectives with technical constraints throughout.

The result wasn't simply a new internal tool. It freed support staff to focus on supporting users and cut the time it took customers to get a response — happier staff, happier partners and happier customers.

The best product work isn't always customer-facing. Sometimes the biggest impact comes from removing friction behind the scenes — giving teams the tools they need to work faster and allowing them to spend more time creating value for customers.

Impact

A product ecosystem, not just a storefront.

What started as a webshop audit became a multi-year foundation supporting Native Instruments' commercial platform, its brand portfolio and the partners publishing into it.

Project 02 — Forrit

Designing an enterprise CMS around the people using it.

Forrit develops enterprise CMS software used by organisations with complex publishing needs. What began as a defined engagement has grown into an ongoing partnership of close to two years, embedded alongside their product and delivery teams.

The challenge wasn't creating a visually impressive interface — it was designing a product that balanced the needs of clients, delivery teams and engineers, while remaining scalable as the platform evolved.

The Approach

Building within Material UI, not around it.

Working within the constraints of Material UI, I helped extend and refine the product's design language without sacrificing usability or brand identity. Material UI became a foundation rather than a constraint — allowing new functionality to feel familiar, consistent and easier to build.

Reducing cognitive load across complex workflows

Every feature was approached from the user's perspective — questioning interactions, simplifying navigation, improving content hierarchy, refining language and restructuring journeys to make difficult tasks feel considerably easier.

Working closely with delivery teams and engineers meant solutions could be validated early, balancing user needs with technical feasibility while continually evolving the product through iterative improvements.

The result was a platform that became easier to learn, more efficient to use and significantly more consistent as new functionality was introduced.

Impact

Consistency that held as the platform grew.

The design system work meant new features could ship without eroding the coherence of the product — a constraint most enterprise platforms lose as they scale.

How I Work

Large organisations rarely struggle for lack of talent.

They struggle because products evolve faster than systems, teams become disconnected, and every new feature introduces more inconsistency. My role is often to connect those moving parts.

Every organisation is different, but the role I play is often the same: bringing clarity where products, people and priorities have become disconnected. Sometimes that means facilitating workshops and shaping strategy. Other times it's designing systems, improving workflows or working alongside engineering to turn ideas into products that scale. The deliverables change. The objective doesn't.

That flexibility works both ways. I'm equally comfortable embedded within a larger design and product team — as I was at Native Instruments and Forrit, working alongside PMs, engineers and delivery leads — or brought in as the sole designer on a project, owning research, design and stakeholder conversations end to end. The size of the team changes; the approach doesn't.

  • Create products that are easier to use
  • Build systems that are easier to maintain
  • Help teams make better decisions, faster

The best design systems don't just make interfaces more consistent — they make organisations more effective. They reduce duplication, speed up delivery, improve collaboration and create better experiences for customers. That's why I enjoy working on them. They're never just about design. They're about helping businesses scale with confidence.
